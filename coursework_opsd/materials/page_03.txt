2.2. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)
has emerged as a popular approach for post-training large
language models, particularly on tasks with easily verifi-
able outcomes such as mathematics and coding, using al-
gorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) (Schul-
man et al., 2017) and Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024).

While RLVR methods have demonstrated strong empirical
performance, they face two key limitations: (1) the reward
signal is sparse, providing only sequence-level feedback
rather than token-level guidance on where errors occur, and
(2) when all sampled responses receive identical rewards
(all correct or all incorrect), the advantages become zero,
preventing any policy update despite the computational cost
of sampling.
